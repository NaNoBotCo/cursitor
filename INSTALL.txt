CURSITOR — HOW TO SET IT UP ON A MAC

Cursitor is a set of tools for a court case: it counts deadlines and shows the arithmetic, makes pleading paper, numbers exhibits, and reads PDFs. It runs on your Mac.

You need: a Mac, about 15 minutes, and this folder.

STEP 1. Put the folder somewhere you will find it.

Drag the "cursitor" folder into your Documents folder.

STEP 2. Open Terminal.

Press Command and Space together, type Terminal, press Return. A window with text opens.

STEP 3. Install the two parts Cursitor uses.

Copy this line, paste it into Terminal, press Return:

python3 -m pip install --user pypdf reportlab

If the Mac asks to install "command line developer tools", click Install and wait. Then run the line again.

STEP 4 (for scanned papers). Install the reading tools.

This step lets Cursitor read scans. It needs Homebrew (brew.sh). With Homebrew installed, paste this line and press Return:

brew install ocrmypdf tesseract ghostscript poppler

Skip this step if your PDFs already have text you can select.

STEP 5. Open the Desk.

In Finder, open the cursitor folder and double-click "Cursitor.command".

The first time, macOS may say it cannot open it. Right-click the file, choose Open, then click Open.

Your web browser opens a page with six big buttons.

THE SIX BUTTONS

Paste a lawsuit — paste text copied from a PDF or a court website. Cursitor removes the line numbers, page numbers and headers, and joins the lines back into paragraphs.

Add PDFs — choose your exhibits. Cursitor puts them in order, adds an "EXHIBIT 1" page before each, stamps a number on every page, and makes one PDF plus a list.

Make pleading paper — paste your text, give it a title. Cursitor makes a PDF on court pleading paper with numbered lines.

What's due — pick the day you were served, how you were served, and what was served. Cursitor shows the due date and the arithmetic that got there, with the law it used.

Ask a PDF — choose a PDF and type a few words. Cursitor shows where they appear, by page and line.

Send — your recent results. Show one in Finder, copy where it is, or open a Mail message with it attached. You address the message and press Send yourself.

TO STOP

Close the browser tab. In the Terminal window that opened, press Control and C.

THE DATES

Every date comes with its arithmetic, for example: "Due Wed Sep 9 2026 — served by email Aug 5 + 30 days = Fri Sep 4; + 2 court days for e-service, skipping Labor Day = Wed Sep 9."

Court holidays come from tables in the "holidays" folder. Courts can close on other days. Your court's own calendar is the one that counts.

A date shows SCHEDULED when a court order, notice or proof of service for it is saved in your case folder. Otherwise it shows PROPOSED.
